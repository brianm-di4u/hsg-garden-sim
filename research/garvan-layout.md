# Garvan Woodland Gardens: layout, features and history

Research notes for *Woodland Gardens Keeper*. Collected 2026-11-01 from the sources listed under each section.
The official garden map PDF could not be fetched, so relative positions come from OpenStreetMap coordinates, the
garden's own venue pages and visitor walk-throughs. Lines marked **(uncertain)** disagree between sources or rest on
one informal source. Fact cards in `data/facts.json` quote this file (`corpus` checks in `tools/check-facts.mjs`).

## Site

- Garvan Woodland Gardens is a 210-acre woodland botanical garden on a forested peninsula in Lake Hamilton, with
  about 4.5 miles of shoreline. Address: 550 Arkridge Road, Hot Springs, Arkansas.
- It is owned by the University of Arkansas and run through the Fay Jones School of Architecture and Design.
- About 44 acres are the developed tour area; the rest is woodland of pine, oak, hickory and ash, with ravines,
  streams and waterfalls in the Ouachita Mountains.
- Visitors arrive from the east on Arkridge Road. The Hixson Family Nature Preserve is on the north side of the
  peninsula; the westernmost point is the Perry Wildflower Overlook on the lake.
- The garden says the Perry Wildflower Overlook is nearly half a mile from the main entrance.
- Paths in the tour area total about three miles. The only named trails found: a 1.5-mile shoreline walking trail
  (designed 2003 by Liz Frazier), the Birdsong Trail in the Hixson preserve, a quarter-mile camellia trail, and
  "Woodland Walk" (photo captions).

## Areas and structures

- **Pratt Welcome Center.** Wood and native stone, designed by French Harris of Hot Springs. Houses the entry
  gate, the GardenShop, golf cart rentals and the Magnolia Room.
- **Weyerhaeuser Bonsai Garden and Learning Center.** Next to the entrance; nearly three dozen bonsai and penjing.
- **Garden of the Pine Wind.** A Japanese garden of about 4 acres in a natural ravine, designed with David Slawson.
  It has three cascades, two springs, four pools, a 12-foot waterfall and a half-acre koi pond with a meadow beside
  it (Koi Pond Meadow). Bridges: the Joy Manning Scott Bridge of the Full Moon (stone, 11 feet high, with a circular
  opening); the Sunrise Bridge, named by the mayor of Hanamaki, Japan, Hot Springs' sister city; and the Floating
  Cloud Bridge, where white azaleas are clipped to look like clouds.
- **Verna C. Garvan Pavilion.** By E. Fay Jones and Maurice Jennings: open-air, sandstone and redwood, with a glass
  roof rising above a central oculus shaped like an unfolding flower. It sits among Mrs. Garvan's original
  plantings, a short walk from the Welcome Center. Completion year not found.
- **Anthony Chapel.** Opened in September 2006, designed by Maurice Jennings and David McKee: wood, glass and steel
  with yellow-pine beams and columns, a 57-foot ceiling and floor-to-ceiling windows. The complex sits outside the
  ticketed area next to the main parking lot. The Anthony Family Carillon is a bell tower of 16 copper-clad
  columns. Millsap Bride's Hall is south of the chapel; Evans Groom's Quarters is northeast of it; the Evans
  Celebration Garden is a two-level round lawn with a waterfall next to the Bride's Hall. **(uncertain)** Seating is
  given as 160, 180 or 200; the carillon's height as 55, 57 or 59 feet.
- **Evans Children's Adventure Garden.** 1.5 acres with a cave behind a 12-foot waterfall, a rock maze, climbing
  boulders, wading pools, an iron bridge shaped like woven branches and a 450-foot elevated walkway.
- **Bob and Sunny Evans Tree House.** By Modus Studio of Fayetteville, in steel and heat-treated pine; soft opening
  June 30, 2018. A small seasonal Fairy Village display is a short walk away.
- **Hixson Family Nature Preserve.** 45 acres of dense forest on the north side, behind the Treehouse, with the
  Birdsong Trail and bird feeding stations.
- **Millsap Canopy Bridge.** A 120-foot winding walkway on stone piers about two stories up, crossing Singing
  Springs Gorge (cascades, pools, ferns).
- **Perry Wildflower Overlook.** The westernmost point: a flagstone terrace over Lake Hamilton beside a wildflower
  meadow, with restrooms.
- **Sugg Model Train Garden.** Behind the Welcome Center: G-scale trains on 389 feet of track, with miniatures of
  Malvern Brick & Tile and the Wisconsin-Arkansas Lumber mill, both Cook and Garvan family businesses.
- **Paul W. and Valerie Klipsch Amphitheater.** In the middle of the gardens, in a natural hollow, with flagstone
  seating for 500 under tall pines, oaks and hickories.
- Other named places on official pages: Dooley Pergola (near the Welcome Center), Southern Inspiration Garden,
  Sensory Garden, Ellen Edmondson Great Lawn, Dierks Promenade, a flowering border, a conifer border and rock
  gardens. Exact positions not found **(uncertain)**; the game places them plausibly.
- Not found anywhere (left out of the game): a Klein Celebration Garden, a Hixson Asian Garden, a Joplin bonsai
  garden, a daylily promenade, Chipmunk Hollow, a magnolia garden, a fragrance garden, an Elkins bird sanctuary,
  a boathouse. A boat dock on the lake was a cart-tour stop but has been removed.

## Plant collections

- More than 160 varieties of azaleas (one source counts over 2,000 azalea plants), especially in the Garden of the
  Pine Wind.
- About 150,000 tulips of about 150 varieties for the Tulip Extravaganza (2021–2026 figures).
- Tens of thousands of daffodils open the season.
- Camellias: about 100 plants of 50 cultivars along a quarter-mile camellia trail; Garvan is on the American
  Camellia Trail.
- Japanese maples, tree peonies and more than 300 Asian ornamentals in the Garden of the Pine Wind.
- Heat-tolerant rhododendrons, hydrangeas, dogwoods and cinnamon ferns in the ravines around the Canopy Bridge.
- Magnolias and roses among Mrs. Garvan's original plantings; an English-style mixed border by the pavilion; a
  conifer border; rock gardens; a wildflower meadow at Perry Point.

## Peafowl

- Official garden photo captions name "George the peacock." Arkansas Tourism's birding article says George can be
  found roaming the grounds, usually near the Pratt Welcome Center.
- A 2019 visitor met a very noisy peacock by the Chipmunk Café and the train garden; a visitor review mentions a
  peahen. **(uncertain)** No official count or history was found. The game has George and two peahens.

## History

- The land was clear-cut around 1915. In the 1920s Arthur B. Cook of Malvern bought it for timber for his
  Wisconsin-Arkansas Lumber mill. Carpenter Dam then created Lake Hamilton, which made the land a peninsula.
- Verna Cook Garvan (1911–1993), Cook's daughter, began the garden in 1956 and developed it over about 40 years.
  She was self-taught and laid out the paths and chose the plants herself.
- In 1985 she signed a trust agreement giving the property to the University of Arkansas landscape architecture
  program. She died in 1993.
- Construction of the public garden began in March 2000, when it was renamed Garvan Woodland Gardens. It was
  dedicated on April 7, 2002.
- More than 125,000 visitors come each year.

## Visitor services (official site, 2026)

- Open daily 9 AM to 6 PM; closed Thanksgiving, Christmas and all of January, and in severe weather.
- Chipmunk Café: daily 10 AM to 3 PM, behind the train garden.
- GardenShop in the Welcome Center. Guided golf cart rides for people with limited mobility.
- Dogs are welcome on a leash of 6 feet or less, but not in the chapel, the treehouse, the ponds or during
  Holiday Lights. Walking only: no bikes or skateboards.
- Wedding sites include the chapel, the pavilion, Perry Point, Koi Pond Meadow, Dooley Pergola, the Southern
  Inspiration Garden, the Celebration Garden and the Magnolia Room.
