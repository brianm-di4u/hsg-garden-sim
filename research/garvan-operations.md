# Garvan Woodland Gardens: seasonal calendar and operations

Research notes for *Woodland Gardens Keeper*, collected 2026-11-01. The University of Arkansas news site refused
direct fetches, so items cited to it come from search-result summaries. **(uncertain)** marks single or informal
sources; **(none found)** marks questions with no published answer, where the game uses general Southern garden
practice instead. Fact cards in `data/facts.json` quote this file.

## The year at the garden

- **January.** The garden is closed for all of January and reopens about February 1.
- **Daffodil Days (February to March).** Crocus, hyacinths and tens of thousands of daffodils bloom first after
  the garden reopens; camellias and star magnolias bloom in the same late-winter window.
- **Tulip Extravaganza (about March 1 to April 15).** About 150,000 tulips of about 150 varieties; the garden's
  second-busiest season after Holiday Lights. The tulips are hand-planted in designed shapes marked out with string
  lines, with a new theme each year. The 2026 show peaked around March 10 to 12.
- **April.** After the tulips, azaleas, dogwoods and redbuds bloom through the end of April.
- **Summer.** Roses and hydrangeas bloom in late May and June (hydrangeas at Singing Springs Gorge), daylilies in
  June and July. Programs are mostly small: day camps, night hikes, photo walks, workshops.
- **Fall in the Woodlands (September 1 to mid-November).** Japanese maples, chrysanthemums and fall annuals; older
  years featured more than 8,000 mums.
- **Celebrate Fall.** A one-day October festival with the garden decorated with mums, pumpkins and gourds.
- **Trash Bash.** An annual Lake Hamilton shoreline, road and ditch cleanup in late September or early October.
- **Fall color** is expected to peak in the first half of November, with Japanese maples giving the reds
  **(uncertain)**.
- **Holiday Lights (mid or late November to December 31).** 2026 dates: November 21 to December 31. Night-only,
  about 5 to 9:30 PM. Closed Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day. Free hot chocolate; the Chipmunk Café is open
  nightly. No dogs. Light counts conflict: 4 million (2014), 4.5 million over about 18 acres, and "over 7 million"
  (2023); the 2026 site says "millions."
- **Named Holiday Lights pieces.** The Rose Tree, a roughly 50-foot tree with a 12 to 15 minute musical light show;
  the Rose Tree Gallery with two more 40-foot trees, added in 2024; Color Blossoms; an ice tunnel. The display
  follows a five-year plan with additions each year.

## Behind the scenes

- **Holiday Lights installation (2026 contract).** A University of Arkansas bid (IFB05142026, awarded July 1, 2026)
  covers installing and removing the display: installation begins around September 15 across about 20 acres, with
  about 150 pole trees, about 60 wrapped trees, about 35,000 square feet of wrapped shrubs and about 300 pre-lit set
  pieces and hanging ornaments, plus electrical work and safety measures.
- An interim executive director described the show as about 46 weeks of work a year (2024). Elements can weigh over
  300 pounds and the work involves a lot of climbing. Volunteers cannot operate machinery, for liability reasons.
- **Teardown** timing was not published **(none found)**; the game tears down during the January closure.
- **Tulip planting.** The tulips are treated as annuals and replanted every year: the area is now USDA Zone 8, too
  warm for most tulips to come back. Staff moved planting later, into January, so tulips would not bloom while the
  garden is closed. For the 2026 show the crew planted 150,000 tulips in 10 days, a garden record.
- **Tulip weather.** Brief dips below freezing are fine, but a sustained hard freeze in the mid-20s °F damages the
  blooms.
- **What happens to tulips after bloom** was not published **(none found)**; the game pulls them in late spring and
  replants the beds with summer annuals.
- **Mulching, pruning, leaves, invasives, irrigation** routines were not published **(none found)**. Game defaults
  from general Southern woodland-garden practice: prune azaleas right after they bloom; prune Japanese maples in
  winter; mulch with pine straw; clear leaves from November to January; clear invasive privet in winter.
- **Storms.** In 2012 the garden closed for the rest of the year after a winter storm and power outages.
- **Volunteers** apply through the garden; there is a Volunteer of the Year award.

## Weddings and programs

- The garden hosts about 200 weddings a year (University of Arkansas release; a TV segment said "almost 200").
- Concerts are held in Anthony Chapel, including free holiday concerts in December.

## How the game maps the calendar

Four seasons of 28 game days each (Spring = March–May, Summer = June–August, Fall = September–November,
Winter = December–February), so one game day stands for about three and a quarter real days. This mapping is a
game choice.
